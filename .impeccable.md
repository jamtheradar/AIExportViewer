## Design Context

### Users
Non-technical managers (HR, team leads, compliance, business owners) reviewing how their staff use Claude. They open an organisation export on their own PC, look at who used it and how much, read conversations, check for sensitive data, and flag things for follow-up. Sessions can be long and involve a lot of reading. The material is sensitive: staff conversations, possibly personal or confidential information. Users are not technical and should never feel they need to be.

### Brand Personality
Crisp, professional, dependable. It should feel like a well-made piece of business software that gets out of the way: efficient, clear and calm about serious material. No brand colours to follow; the palette is ours to choose.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Feel like:** Xero / MYOB (friendly business software for non-technical people) and Microsoft 365 / Outlook (familiar office tool, Windows-native feel).
- **Not like:** a developer tool. Avoid the dark "console" sidebar, monospace used for ordinary content, raw IDs and paths in Basic view, jargon. Also not a flashy dashboard: no hero numbers, gradients or decorative charts.
- **Theme:** Light by default, whatever the computer is set to. Dark is an option in Settings for those who want it.
- **Fonts:** the computer's installed fonts only (Segoe UI on Windows, the system font on Mac). The privacy rules forbid remote fonts.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA contrast. Larger base text than the original 14px, for long reading sessions and older eyes. Colour never carries meaning on its own (status badges pair colour with a shape and a label).

### Design Principles
1. **Content first, chrome second.** Conversations, names and findings are what matter; navigation and frames should be quiet.
2. **Obvious at a glance.** One clear main action per screen; the important number or finding is the first thing the eye lands on.
3. **Office-familiar, not technical.** Plain-English labels, familiar controls, nothing that looks like code unless it is code (Expert view).
4. **Serious without being cold.** Sensitive material is handled with restraint: calm colours, warnings that are clear but not alarming.
5. **Readable for hours.** Comfortable text size, line length and spacing; enough contrast without glare.
